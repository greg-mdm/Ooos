# Landing the coin skate: one video, three finals

**Written:** 2026-09-17, from frame grabs of the three finals in this folder
and on Drive. Supersedes the multi-cam cut in `MASHUP-PLAN-coin-skate.md`,
which was built on the earlier takes; the lighting rules and the wordmark
rules from it still apply.

**Deliverable:** one continuous piece, about 34 seconds, 21:9, no text,
that plays once on the CID banner and holds its final frame. Assembled in
Artlist Studio from the three finals, with one bridge to close the gap in
the Hockey Stop.

- **Aspect.** Parts 1 and 2 are 21:9. The Hockey Stop is a 1920x1080 file,

  nothing; then scale to 2206x946 to match.
- **Light.** Parts 1 and 2 are the Eurovision stage. The Hockey Stop is the
  moody teal. Treated below as a deliberate lights-down for the last move.

## The narrative

1. **The performance.** The coin takes the ice and carves its circle. Part 1.
2. **The signature.** It carves again, harder, and the rise reveals the
   Ooo! in the circle. Part 2. The big finish of the routine.
3. **The stop.** Lights down. The coin comes back to centre and stops
   short: the hockey stop, the spray, and the rest on the skyline face.
   This is the banner's final frame, where the page sets its last line in
   red on the white snow.

The stop after the signature is the right order. The reveal is the flourish;
the stop is the full stop, and the page needs it as its final frame.

## Closing the gap in the Hockey Stop

The black runs 4.95 to 5.85 s, 0.9 s. On either side of it the coin is at
the same spot, centre ice, which is the good news. What changes is its
attitude: at 4.90 s it is on its edge, seen edge-on, finishing a curve
back to centre with its trail behind it; at 5.90 s it has turned its face
toward the camera and is starting to tip into the stop, blurred. Cutting
the black out and butting the two frames together makes the coin snap
through a quarter turn. Three fixes, in the order to try them:

1. **Generate the bridge.** Seedance 2.5, 2 seconds, Start Frame
   `bridge-A-4.90s.png`, End Frame `bridge-B-5.90s.png` (both exported
   from the clip into this folder at full frame). Prompt:

   > The gold coin, upright on its edge at centre ice, turns a quarter
   > turn to face the camera as it decelerates and begins to tip sideways
   > into a hockey stop, the CN Tower face coming round, teal arena rail
   > lights, locked camera at ice level, black ice reflections with the
   > carved trail behind it, no snow yet, no text.

   Use only the middle second of the result if its ends drift from the
   frames. This is the fix that reads as one take.
2. **Flash across it.** Cut 4.90 to 5.90, and put a four-frame white flash
   on the cut, a strobe like the rest of the piece. Because the coin does
   not move, only turns, the flash covers it well. Costs nothing, and it
   may be all this needs.
3. **Freeze and slide.** Hold 4.90 for 12 frames with a 15 percent
   push-in, then cut. A fallback, not a first choice.

Do not use a crossfade. Two coins in two places dissolving into each
other is the one thing that would read as a mistake.

## Continuity between the worlds

- Grade the first three seconds of the Hockey Stop (timeline 24.5 to 27.5)
  with a magenta wash fading to nothing, so the concert lights go down
  rather than switch off. A stock light-leak on a second track, Screen
  blend, 50 percent, fading out, does the same job faster.
- Match the blacks: Parts 1 and 2 have a lifted, purple-tinted floor; the
  Hockey Stop's floor is deeper. Lift the Hockey Stop's shadows a touch.
- Keep the final frame clean. No overlay, no light leak, no strobe on the
  rest. The page writes on it.
