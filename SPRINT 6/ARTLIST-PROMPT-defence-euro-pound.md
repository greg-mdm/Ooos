# Artlist prompt: the Defence clip, a € coin and a £ coin

**Written:** 2026-09-18, revised the same evening. Greg's call: not the
real coin faces, the currency symbols. Two gold coins as they are, each
with one clear symbol etched into its face, € on one and £ on the other.
For the Toolkit, video-to-video (or the AI Agent with the clip attached),
against `SPRINT 6/Defence - Defence - Allies are forming.mp4` (9.9 s,
2560x1080, two gold coins skating backwards side by side, the hockey stop
and the spray at the end).

## What to attach

1. The Defence clip.
2. `coin-refs/symbol-euro-2000px.png` and `coin-refs/symbol-pound-2000px.png`:
   the two glyphs, black on white, Georgia Bold, so the model has the
   exact shapes. Optional; the prompt names them in words as well.

The real-coin photographs in `coin-refs/` are from the earlier direction
and are not needed for this run.

## The prompt

> Keep this video exactly as it is: the same two gold coins skating
> backwards side by side on the black ice, the same speed, path, spacing,
> size, gold metal, lighting, teal arena rail lights, reflections, camera
> and framing, the same hockey stop and spray of snow at the end. Change
> only what is engraved on the face of each coin.
>
> Each coin's face becomes plain polished gold with one large currency
> symbol etched into it, centred, filling most of the face, deep and
> crisp, like a minted relief: the left coin carries the euro sign €, the
> right coin carries the pound sign £. Nothing else on either face: no
> skyline, no tower, no maple leaf, no lettering, no year, no stars, no
> rim text. The edge of each coin stays a plain milled gold edge.
>
> The etched symbol catches the same light the gold does, bright on its
> raised edges and dark in its cut, and stays locked to the coin as it
> turns, skates and stops; it never slides, swims, mirrors or swaps
> between coins. Both coins stay solid gold, one metal, no silver centre.
> No other coin appears. No text anywhere in the frame beyond the two
> symbols. No change to the ice, the rink, the snow or the timing.

## Negative

> No extra coins, no coin changing shape, no second symbol on a face, no
> lettering or numerals, no silver or two-tone metal, no floating or
> flickering symbol, no colour shift in the arena, no slow motion, no
> cut, no zoom.

## Notes for the run

- Left and right are as the viewer sees them. Swap the two symbols in the
  sentence if Greg wants the pound on the left.
- If a run puts the symbol on the edge or the back, add: "the symbol is
  on the face that turns toward the camera during the stop."
- If a run keeps the skyline under the symbol, add: "the face has nothing
  on it but the symbol; remove the skyline and the tower entirely."
- If the symbol reads as painted rather than cut, add: "engraved relief,
  not paint; the symbol has depth and its edges catch the light."
- Mirror check: the € has its bars on the left and its opening on the
  right; the £ has its crossbar low and its tail at the bottom right. If a
  run flips one, say which and ask for it the right way round.

## Pass 2: metal and light (2026-09-19)

The first run (Seedance 2.0, 1080p, six seconds) came back with the
symbols in place but the faces reading as flat animation, not metal. The
third coin in the frame, the one skating forward edge-on between the
two, is part of the shot and stays; its face never shows, so it needs
nothing. This pass is a video-to-video run on that result (attach the
six-second clip, not the Defence original). It says nothing about the
symbols except to keep them. It is all about the surface and the light.

> Keep this video as it is: all three coins, the two € and £ faces, the
> coin skating forward between them, their paths, speed, spacing, size,
> the ice, the arena, the camera, the hockey stop and the spray. Change
> only the material of the coins and the way light plays on them.
>
> The coins are solid minted gold, photographed, not drawn: a polished
> metal face with a soft mirror sheen, faint concentric brushing from the
> strike, a milled edge that catches light as a bright thin line, and
> the etched symbol standing as real relief, its raised edges bright and
> its cut floor darker, with a hard specular highlight that travels along
> the relief as the coin turns.
>
> The light on the coins moves. As they skate backwards past the teal
> rail lights, reflections of the rink sweep smoothly across each face,
> streaks of teal and white sliding over the gold in step with the
> skating; the face brightens and dims as it turns toward and away from
> the lights, the way real metal does, with no flat unchanging fill. The
> gold reads as warm metal: deep amber in the shadows, pale near-white in
> the hottest highlight, never yellow paint. Each coin throws a warm
> reflection onto the black ice beneath it that moves with it.
>
> Sharp, physically lit, film-realistic, the metal reacting to every
> change of angle. No cartoon shading, no outline, no flat colour, no
> glow, no sparkle effects, no change to the symbols, the motion or the
> timing.

### Negative

> No coin added or removed, no flat shading, no matte or plastic surface,
> no painted-on symbol, no outlines, no bloom or lens sparkle, no colour
> shift in the arena, no change to the two symbols, the coins' paths or
> the cut.

### Notes for the run

- Video-to-video with the six-second result attached, at a lower change
  strength than pass 1: the geometry is right, only the surface needs to
  move. If the Toolkit exposes a strength or adherence slider, keep it
  toward "follow the source".
- If the Toolkit offers Seedance 2.5 and the credits allow, this pass is
  the one to spend them on: the 2.0 result is where the flat metal came
  from.
- If the faces still read flat, the single most useful line is: "a hard
  specular highlight that travels across the face as the coin turns."
  Moving highlight is what the eye reads as metal.
- If the model overdoes it and the coins turn into mirrors, add: "soft
  sheen, brushed, not a mirror; the reflections are streaks, not images."
- If the symbols drift in this pass, drop the strength a notch rather
  than re-describing them; pass 1 already got them right.
