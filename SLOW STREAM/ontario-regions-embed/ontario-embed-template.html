Land acknowledgement

Slow Stream Studio sits on Indigenous land in Toronto, Ontario, Canada.

“Tkarón” (Toronto) belongs to the Dish with One Spoon territory, which is the traditional territory of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, the Wendat, and the Mississaugas of the Credit First Nation.

  • Native Land ↗ A global resource mapping Indigenous territories, treaties, and languages, with a focus on Indigenous-led representation, community, and education.

  • Whose land are you on? ↗ This web-based app uses GIS technology to help people identify Indigenous Nations, territories and communities across Canada.

46 treaties and agreements cover the territory now called Ontario.

The Dish with One Spoon territory is the result of a treaty between the Six Nations Confederacy, the Anishinaabek, and allied nations to peaceably share and care for this land, its waters, and all the biodiversity in the Great Lakes region. Everyone that settles here is called to uphold this treaty peacefully, building friendship and mutual respect.

Our Shadowy Backstory

A Remix of a Remix

Singing in the Shadows (2026) is cover art in every sense: a second-generation remix of Shadow, the print-on-demand release of McCormack’s abstract painting from Canvases of Old Dreams (2006–2026).

For over 20 years, we’ve traded media back and forth, from paintings to pixels and back again. McCormack’s canvas gives the sheet ghost colour and form. Admittedly, my media aesthetic gives Shadow a presence in a new, unreal environment.

The receding floor lines and the small figure in the distance reimagine the “Broadway Melody” ballet in Singin’ in the Rain (1952), a beautifully surreal scene from a film about a studio faking its own voice.1

The bubbles floated out of Jean Siméon Chardin’s Soap Bubbles (c. 1733/1734). In European art, bubbles often suggest life is fleeting, and fun is frivolous. Yet Chardin’s figures show deep concentration. The painting may be about learning the world through experience.2 Drifting through Singing in the Shadows (2026), they are filled with possibility.

I shot the Toronto skyline with a handheld camera, turned it into a silhouette photo montage, and overlaid the generated image with “11 economic regions.” Shadow’s release is hyped with a remix of a remix.

Now, Shadow is a hyperreal ghost, both figure and sign, waiting to be seen as meanings multiply.

McCormack’s influential painting debuted at the Canadian Interactive Exhibition (CIX). Shadow is available to bring home as a vibrant art piece, printed on museum-quality matte and framed in black wood.

Slow Stream makes it easy to buy directly from artists. Available exclusively through our studio shop, the inaugural edition of “Shadow” is limited to 100 authentic prints. We license artwork for a limited term, after which distribution rights revert to the creator and future releases reflect new appraisal of the work’s value.

Gregory Tyler LongArtistic Director, Slow Stream
